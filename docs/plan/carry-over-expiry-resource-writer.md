# Pending carry-over expiry resource writer

The lifecycle worker retains `expirePendingCarryOvers(limit)` and its numeric expired-count result. The implementation now lives in `queueCarryOverExpiryService`. Its bounded due scan is advisory and takes no ticket locks. Each candidate enters an owned location-first transaction, rereads the exact tenant/location/ticket scope and current expired deadline, then locks the ticket with `SKIP LOCKED`. Changed status, moved scope or extended deadline produces no expiry outcome. Inactive branches can still finish otherwise eligible abandoned work.

Each eligible ticket commits its expired outcome, linked booking fulfillment/refund outcome, unused protection cancellation receipts, scoped lifecycle event, durable notification intents and scope revision together. Linked bookings and protected bindings are locked in ID order after the ticket. Existing booking outcomes and terminal-status exclusions are retained: completed/canceled/reviewed/disputed bookings are not rewritten. Frozen protected bindings cancel after draft mapping changes or tracking disable. The expiry event now includes its location; existing event/idempotency keys and web-push/FCM/email intent policy remain.

The new trusted internal `withCarryOverExpiryTransaction` binds its capability to one selected ticket. It has no fabricated user ID and accepts only `cancelReservation` with `ticket:<ticketId>:reservation:<reservationId>:carry-over-expiry`. The key must match the ticket and payload, and the binding must belong to that scope's linked booking. The ticket must already be expired for the carry-over reason with a due deadline at/before its terminal timestamp. It cannot reserve, allocate, release, cancel another ticket's protection, use customer cancellation keys or use unarrived booking-expiry keys. The existing booking-expiry and customer adapters retain their own restrictions. Valid receipt replay does not advance revision.

Service-start history, any historical/actual allocation, converted protection or a captured protected booking with an inconsistent current ticket link are left for reconciliation. They do not expire through this adapter. Actual allocations are never released, and no explicit service outcome is inferred. Unsupported unsafe numeric persistence IDs fail reconciliation rather than round through event/outbox repositories. Null/missing historical scope and other corrupt source rows still need reconciliation before complete coverage.

Atomicity is per ticket, not across an entire worker batch. A later error can leave earlier ticket transactions committed; retry scans/rereads their terminal states and does not repeat them. A no-op transaction may establish the scope's baseline row but never advances its revision or emits an outcome. A successful expiry advances the domain revision once in addition to each protection command's committed revision. Event, outbox or final revision failure rolls that ticket's domain/protection/receipt/revision state back.

Validation uses actual PostgreSQL ledger, ticket and booking SQL with bounded lifecycle-event/outbox fixtures. It covers concurrent workers, current deadline/status/scope after lock waits, terminal booking preservation, frozen protection after disable/edit, retained occupancy/converted state, orphan captured links, bounded independent scopes, receipt replay, unsafe IDs, event/outbox/final-revision rollback and restricted-capability rejection. The FCM source-contract test now reads both closure and extracted expiry modules. These checks do not claim full production-schema or authenticated live expiry proof.

Enforced/system closure, deletion, catalog/settings authorization/revisions, historical reconciliation and complete ready inventory remain separate gaps. Tracking and writer-coverage constraints remain disabled; producer readiness, capture/export/inference and customer estimates are unchanged.
